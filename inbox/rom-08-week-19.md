---

## Main Point

**"The good" is not** ==comfort== **— it is** ==conformity==.

> Christians don't experience less bad stuff in life than non-Christians. The passage defines its own terms: verse 28 promises "the good," verse 29 immediately defines it as *"to be conformed to the image of His Son."* The good is not a favorable outcome — it is a transformed person.

## Connections

- **Romans 8:29 → Philippians 1:6**: "The good work" God completes (Phil 1:6) is the same "good" of Romans 8:28 — conformity to Christ. These are the same promise from two angles.
- **Romans 8:29 ("foreknew") → Matthew 7:23 ("I never knew you")**: To be *foreknown* by God is to be in relationship, not just selected. The people in Matthew 7 did impressive works but were *unknown*. The contrast is relationship vs. performance.
- **The Great Commission (Matt 28) → "conformed to the image of His Son"**: Discipleship *is* the mechanism of Christlikeness. The mission and the transformation are inseparable.

---

## Things Worth Sitting With

- The Spirit intercedes because **we don't know what to pray for**. This is pastoral — we don't need to have prayer figured out. Weakness in prayer is the assumed starting point, not a failure state.
- The "golden chain" (foreknew → predestined → called → justified → glorified) is written in past tense even for glorification. Paul treats God's future completion as already certain — which is the ground for the comfort, not just the theology.
- Matthew 7:21–23 is jarring here: remarkable religious activity (prophecy, exorcism, miracles) done *in Jesus' name* — and rejected. The issue was never the works. It was being known. This recalibrates what "doing the will of the Father" actually means.

---

## Reformed Assumptions

> These are assumptions commonly baked into how this passage gets read — worth naming explicitly.

1. **"All things work together for good" ≠ things will turn out the way you want.** The passage doesn't promise pleasant circumstances or resolved suffering. It defines "good" as Christlikeness — which often *requires* suffering (cf. Romans 8:17).

2. **"Called according to His purpose" — the purpose is specified.** It's not a vague divine plan for your flourishing. The purpose is conformity to Christ's image (v.29). This narrows the claim considerably.

3. **The Spirit's intercession is not a guarantee your requests are granted.** The Spirit prays *according to the will of God* (v.27), not according to your requests. The comfort is that your prayers aren't wasted even when confused — not that they'll be answered as asked.

4. **Predestination here is about *destination*, not just *selection*.** The predestination in view is being "conformed to the image of His Son" — it's teleological, not merely electoral. The point is *what you're being shaped into*, not just *whether you were chosen*.

5. **Religious activity is not the same as being known.** Matthew 7:21–23 is not a warning about casual Christians — it's about people doing overtly supernatural ministry in Jesus' name. The danger isn't complacency; it's substituting performance for relationship.
